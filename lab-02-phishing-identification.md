# Lab 2 --- Phishing Identification

## Objective

Practice identifying phishing emails by analyzing common technical and
social-engineering indicators.

## Platform

-   TryHackMe
-   TryPhishMe interactive training environment

## Concepts Learned

-   Urgency and scare tactics
-   Look-alike sender addresses
-   Display-name impersonation
-   Malicious attachments
-   Compromised email accounts
-   Suspicious requests
-   Too-good-to-be-true offers

## Practical Work

I completed an interactive phishing-identification exercise with
realistic email scenarios. For each scenario, I checked the sender
address, domain spelling, display name, urgency, attachments, requests
for sensitive information, and suspicious offers before deciding whether
the email was phishing.

**Safety:** All activity was performed only inside the authorized
TryHackMe training environment.

## Result

Successfully completed the training and obtained the required lab flag.

**Flag:** `THM{i_phish_you_not}`

## Key Takeaways

-   A familiar display name does not prove an email is genuine.
-   Small domain changes can make fake addresses look legitimate.
-   Urgency is commonly used to pressure users.
-   Unexpected attachments and sensitive-data requests are important
    warning signs.
-   Multiple clues should be considered together rather than treating
    one clue as proof.
